Making one

You'll need

  • A printer
  • Scissors: the only cutting tool you need, windows included. A clean, straight cut matters most for the Classic Edition.
  • Pocket Edition only: a brass paper fastener (split pin)
  • Sleeve Edition only: a glue stick
  • Optional: cardstock, to make it sturdier

Print at 100%

Not "fit to page," so everything comes out full size. Each sheet has a print check at the bottom: a line that measures 50 mm. The sheets are US Letter size but print fine on A4.

The assembly steps are printed on each sheet. Once it's built, try C up to D: the main chords, C, F, G, and Am, become D, G, A, and Bm. It works for other chords too: Em becomes F#m, and G7 becomes A7. Use it for a while and you may start to notice a pattern or two.

For teachers

Slide-a-Key makes the structure of transposition visible. The strips show that transposing is a fixed distance; the wheel shows that the twelve notes form a loop. Students can find both patterns with their hands before anyone names them.

They make it

Cutting, folding, and fastening turn a worksheet into an object. The first time the notes line up, it's their own tool that worked.

They discover it

Hold back the explanation. A student who notices every note moved two boxes has found the idea of an interval for themselves.

They keep it

Let students sign it, colour in the sharps, and keep it in an instrument case. A tool they reach for next week keeps teaching.

The teacher's guide has a 45-minute lesson, nine discovery questions with answers, and ideas for transposing instruments, capos, and singing range.
