Making one

You'll need

  • A printer
  • Scissors, or a craft knife and ruler. A clean, straight cut matters most for the Tent Edition.
  • Pocket Edition only: a brass paper fastener (split pin)
  • Optional: cardstock, to make it sturdier

Print at 100%

Not "fit to page." The note boxes have to stay the same size so the strips and rings line up. On the Classic and Tent sheets, one full note strip measures exactly 7 inches.

The assembly steps are printed on each sheet. Once it's built, try C up to F: C becomes F, E becomes A, and G becomes C. It works for chords too: Am becomes Dm, and G7 becomes C7. Use it for a while and you may start to notice a pattern or two.

For teachers

Slide-a-Key makes the structure of transposition visible. The strips show that transposing is a fixed distance; the wheel shows that the twelve notes form a loop. Students can find both patterns with their hands before anyone names them.

They make it

Cutting, folding and fastening turn a worksheet into an object. The first time the notes line up, it's their own tool that worked.

They discover it

Hold back the explanation. A student who notices every note moved five boxes has found the idea of an interval for themselves.

They keep it

Let students sign it, colour in the sharps and keep it in an instrument case. A tool they reach for next week keeps teaching.

The teacher's guide has a 45-minute lesson, six discovery questions with answers, and ideas for transposing instruments, capos and singing range.
